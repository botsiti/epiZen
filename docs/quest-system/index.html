Epizen / Quest system / Design proposal 01

A calmer kind
of progress.

Give your effort a visible history. Give your goals a satisfying finish. Let every life area tell its own story.

9 November 2026 · Proposed rules and settings, not an installed app update.

The whole model in one sentence

Earn 1 XP for each intentional minute, add a modest bonus for a defined outcome, and distribute that XP across the life areas it serves.

01 / Three separate ideas

Time, achievement, direction.

01 · Task categoryWhat did I do?

Kaizen, business, sport, reading. Categories organize activity and recorded minutes.

02 · QuestWhat changed?

A finished chapter, a shipped release, twelve completed sessions. A quest defines an observable outcome.

03 · Life sphereWhat did it serve?

Personal Growth, Career/Work, Health/Fitness. Spheres receive a share of XP, never an extra copy.

“Kaizen” is a category, “Finish a course project” is a quest, and “Personal Growth” is a destination. A task can earn time XP without belonging to any quest. Linking it to a quest does not pay its minutes again.

Total XP = recorded minutes + earned quest bonuses

Time XP: 1 minute = 1 XP

Use the same rate for all intentional activities, including recovery. A 10-minute meditation earns 10 XP; 60 minutes of coding earns 60; a deliberately chosen 120-minute film earns 120.

“Intentional” means an activity you chose to record. Passive background playback, scheduled time and unattended timers do not become effort automatically. Ordinary care and recovery count; XP is a record of attention, not economic output.

Completion bonus: 20–30%

B = round(E × 0.20 × D)

E is the estimated active effort in minutes, fixed when the quest starts. D is 1 for familiar work, 1.25 for a stretch, or 1.5 for unfamiliar work with meaningful uncertainty. Only the bonus uses difficulty.

For a ten-hour stretch quest: 600 minutes of work + 150 bonus XP = 750 XP when actual effort matches the estimate.

Why this is mathematically useful

  • Additive: splitting 60 minutes into six sessions still gives 60 XP. There is no reward for creating extra checkboxes.
  • Conservative routing: 70% + 30% distributes 100% of the reward. Adding a sphere cannot create XP.
  • Predictable: bonuses are 20–30% of planned effort. If estimates are accurate, outcomes contribute roughly 17–23% of total XP.
  • Stable: no expiry, lateness deductions or streak multipliers. Corrections can reverse a mistaken award; a difficult week cannot erase real work.

This bound is against planned effort, not actual effort. A quest finished faster can have a higher bonus-to-time ratio. That is acceptable: freeze an honest estimate, then use estimation errors to improve the next quest. Do not pay an estimate as if it were logged time.

02 / Your activities, your language

Choose destinations by intention.

Keep the existing category names as the starter set. The following routing is a starting suggestion. All categories use 1 XP/min in the proposed model. “Main” and “recovery” remain useful time labels and do not change the rate.

One hour of Kaizen → 60 XP

At 100% Personal Growth, all 60 XP goes there. Change the route to 70% Personal Growth / 30% Career/Work: the same hour becomes 42 + 18 XP. Your character still receives exactly 60.

The eight starter spheres are Career/Work, Health/Fitness, Personal Growth, Finance, Spiritual, Love/Relationship, Fun/Recreation and Social. Give each sphere a description meaningful to you. Finance can mean stewardship of money; Spiritual can mean reflection or meaning, without implying a religious practice.

When a category deserves a different route

A novel read for pleasure can go to Fun/Recreation. A film studied for directing can go to Personal Growth or Career/Work. Meditation may serve Health/Fitness for you. A dog walk may primarily serve health and companionship. Change the category default when that intention is usual; use an explicit per-task override for an exception.

  1. Name the activity you can recognize in a daily note.
  2. Ask what capacity or life area it primarily serves.
  3. Choose one destination first. Add a second only when the benefit is meaningful; divide the existing 100%.
  4. Keep the rate at 1 XP/min. Do not multiply points to make an area seem important. Give it an appropriate weekly intention instead.

Background listening during coding does not create two hours from one. Split overlapping minutes between activities, or choose one primary activity. A time-only manual entry cannot prove overlap; the product should flag suspicious totals and invite correction, not silently discard them.

03 / A clear finish line

Rarity is scale. Difficulty is stretch.

Use planned active effort to suggest rarity. The thresholds below are design choices, not natural laws. A calendar month is a time window, not a difficulty level. Waiting for a year does not earn a year of effort.

RarityEstimated effortTypical outcome
CommonUnder 2 hoursComplete one bounded action
Rare2 to under 10 hoursA small deliverable or several sessions
Epic10 to under 40 hoursA substantial project or milestone
Legendary40+ hoursA major undertaking with several milestones

At exactly 2, 10 and 40 hours, use Rare, Epic and Legendary respectively. Rarity has no additional multiplier. You may choose a different badge for personal significance without changing the numeric reward. Keep “Mythic” as an optional narrative label for a long campaign, not a fifth payout tier.

Difficulty is relative to you

Familiar · 1.00: a known process.
Stretch · 1.25: some new skill or coordination.
Unfamiliar · 1.50: substantial learning or uncertainty.

Set this before starting. Difficulty is not suffering, exhaustion, or danger. A task that becomes easier with practice is a success, not a reason to claw back its reward.

Write acceptance before reward

“Work on business” is an activity. “Publish a usable landing page with contact details and a tested enquiry flow” is a quest. For recurring practice, “Complete twelve 20-minute sessions” is a measurable quest.

Prefer outcomes you can influence: submitting a finished application rather than being hired. Use external results as optional celebrations.

Pay progress through accepted milestones

For a quest with accepted progress p from 0 to 1, cumulative bonus is round(B × p). Pay only the change from the previous cumulative value. For binary quests, p is 0 or 1. For equal deliverables, p = completed / target, clamped to 1. Unequal deliverables need fixed milestone weights summing to 100%.

A ten-hour familiar quest has a 120 XP bonus. At 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%, the cumulative bonus is 30, 60, 90 and 120 XP. Logging effort alone does not assert that a milestone is accepted.

Pause, abandon, or correct a quest

Pausing or abandoning preserves recorded time and bonuses for already accepted milestones. Unfinished milestones pay nothing. Reopening a completed outcome for extra work does not revoke it or grant another full bonus. Correcting a mistaken acceptance explicitly reverses that award, with a preview; the resulting total cannot become negative.

Do not pay the same achievement twice

A campaign and its child quests share one bonus budget. Allocate slices to children; their accepted results release those slices. The parent shows their aggregate and pays no second completion prize. Separate unrelated quests may earn separate bonuses. Repeated identical completion, imports, retries and reopening must never mint a second award.

No estimated effort? Keep it as a checklist goal with a badge and zero bonus until a reasonable estimate is set before work starts. Completed historical goals are not an invitation to manufacture new rewards.

05 / The long game

What is a year of work worth?

Suppose you plan a 240-hour unfamiliar creative project across a year. It is Legendary because of its 240 hours of effort, not because twelve months pass.

Recorded effort, if on plan14,400 XP

240 hours × 60. Earned throughout the year.

Total bonus budget4,320 XP

14,400 × 20% × 1.5. Fixed at the start.

Combined, when complete18,720 XP

Time plus bonus. No extra annual payout.

Divide the outcome into four equally weighted milestones: prototype, complete draft, tested revision, release. Each releases 1,080 bonus XP. If their planned effort differs, use unequal weights fixed at the start. If one quarter has no accepted outcome, time still earns XP; that quarter's bonus waits for acceptance.

Examples assume actual effort equals planned effort and all outcomes are accepted. A monthly 20-hour quest remains Epic. A year-long 10-hour administrative goal is Epic too; duration is not active effort.

Keep the existing level curve

XP to start level L = 500 × L × (L − 1). Levels 2, 3, 5 and 10 begin at 1,000, 3,000, 10,000 and 45,000 XP. The next level costs 1,000 × L XP. This already matches Epizen and needs no extra economy.

Starting from zero, the year-long example reaches level 6: level 6 begins at 15,000 and level 7 at 21,000. At two recorded hours per day with no bonuses, level 2 takes about nine days and level 5 about 84 days. These are pace examples, never daily quotas. Show weekly effort and milestone completion alongside levels as later levels take longer.

06 / Product specification

Two settings homes. One clear boundary.

The current worktree already has a built-in Quests plugin switch. Extend that module; no downloadable plugin runtime, execution of Obsidian scripts, or separate service is needed.

Daily notes → Categories

Own the activity vocabulary: display name, stable tag, Main/Recovery label, order, visibility and whether a row appears in a new daily note. Start with all existing categories, while letting people choose which ones appear.

  • Add: preview the generated #daily/… tag. Validate a nonempty unique tag, case-insensitively; reject whitespace and Markdown delimiters.
  • Rename: change the visible label while keeping the stable tag. A tag rename requires an alias, not a rewrite of old notes.
  • Remove: hide/archive any used category. Delete only never-used definitions. Historical time remains readable.
  • Reorder and hide: affect future templates and selectors. Opening an existing note never inserts or deletes tasks.

Plugins → Quests → Configure

Own the meaning of XP: per-category sphere routing, life-sphere labels and descriptions, quest defaults and the model's effective date. Show the 1 XP/min rule and bonus formula as readable rules, not a wall of tuning sliders.

  • Routing: one main destination by default; optional additional shares totaling exactly 100%. Show “60 minutes → 42 + 18 XP” beside a 70/30 split.
  • Unassigned: new categories with no chosen route retain their XP in a visible Unassigned bucket. Never silently call them Personal Growth.
  • Defaults: Familiar difficulty, no deadline, outcome required, milestone budget shared with parent.
  • Spheres: allow custom names and additions; archive used spheres without losing their history. An active category cannot route to an archived sphere.

A complete configuration interaction

  1. Add “Music practice” in Daily notes, with tag #daily/music-practice. Choose whether it appears in new notes.
  2. Open its Quests routing. Choose Personal Growth 80% and Fun/Recreation 20%.
  3. The preview shows 60 minutes → 48 XP + 12 XP, total 60.
  4. Save both drafts atomically if changed together. A save failure leaves every draft visible with Retry; it never reports success early.
  5. The next 30-minute entry follows the saved route: 24 + 6 XP. Earlier entries keep their original routing policy.

Validation: shares must be finite whole percentages from 0 to 100 and sum to 100, except an explicitly Unassigned route. Duplicate destinations, unknown IDs, negative/nonfinite effort and invalid targets block saving with an inline explanation. Empty categories and unavailable settings need local recovery states. Switching vaults must not apply a late response to the new vault.

Disable Quests: hide its XP and quest surfaces, retain rules and notes, and keep ordinary task/time recording usable. Re-enabling derives XP for recorded time using dated rules, including the disabled interval, without writing rewards again. Quests still requires Periodic notes, matching today's plugin dependency.

Changing policy: use an effective date for category routing and sphere definitions; do not re-score old records. Freeze each quest's estimate, difficulty, budget, routing and milestone weights at creation. Allow scope changes only with an explicit preview of the remaining budget; past accepted rewards stay attached to their original version.

07 / What the numbers can tell you

Read patterns without judging yourself.

Lifetime XP shows accumulated recorded attention and accepted outcomes. It cannot measure skill, health, love, or the value of a person. A high score may mean time invested, detailed logging, or both.

Strengths: look for evidence

Show the last four weeks of minutes by sphere, accepted milestones, and optional personal satisfaction ratings separately. “Consistent practice and three finished pieces” is more informative than “Your strongest trait is creativity.”

Use the same selected date window and show coverage: “18 days with recorded activity.” Missing notes mean unknown, not a failed day.

Gaps: compare with your intentions

If you wanted 90 minutes of movement and logged 30, display “30 of 90 planned minutes.” That is a prompt to adjust next week, not a permanent weakness score.

Do not aim for eight equal spheres. Parenting, a deadline or recovery may legitimately change your priorities. A satisfaction score of zero is different from an unanswered rating.

Understand the tradeoff

A two-hour film earns more time XP than ten minutes of meditation. That is intentional: XP measures recorded attention, while category minutes, personal intentions and milestone outcomes give it meaning. Do not use total XP to rank unlike activities. This is a private, self-reported tracker; it cannot prevent inflated estimates or dishonest logging. Avoid leaderboards, rewards with cash value, and productivity claims based on these totals.

Calibrate for four weeks

Start with the rules above. Once a week, ask whether the model helped you choose meaningful work, made returning easier, or encouraged point chasing. Compare estimated and actual effort for completed quests. If estimates are repeatedly twice reality, improve estimates rather than cutting already-earned XP. If detailed logging feels burdensome, record fewer meaningful sessions.

These are proposed product choices, not a scientifically proven optimal economy. Self-determination theory provides a rationale for choice and useful feedback; it does not establish an XP exchange rate. Self-Determination Theory overview.

Research on gamification reports mixed results and limitations in the evidence. My design inference is to keep rewards transparent, optional and revisable, and evaluate whether they help this user. Neither 20% nor the rarity thresholds are experimentally validated here. Johnson et al., Gamification for health and wellbeing: a systematic review.

No silent nerf on upgrade

Existing vaults stay on Legacy until the user chooses the new model. Preview an explicit boundary date, the old totals and the new rules. Preserve existing quests under Legacy until they are completed or explicitly converted; new quests use the new rules. A conversion credits already-awarded XP and never pays it twice.

Because Legacy penalties change with time, a safe transition must freeze their contribution at the boundary rather than leave old dates changing new totals. Preserve a clearly labeled Legacy opening balance and its per-sphere values; any mismatch belongs in a visible Legacy adjustment bucket. New XP then follows conservation. Do not invent a retrospective sphere distribution to make the old numbers look tidy.

Legacy data remains in ordinary Markdown. Store dated policy in per-vault app settings, owned by the existing settings boundary; provide an explicit human-readable Markdown export/import for portability. No hidden vault sidecars or database. Old notes are not rewritten on opening. Any future optional note edits must use the existing atomic expected-content write path.

Implementation seams and required acceptance evidence

Daily category definitions belong in the category/domain foundation. Pure scoring takes entries and an explicit policy as input. Parsing and Home/periodic rollups must consume that same policy. Quests owns budgets, milestone allocation and accepted progress. Settings owns validated per-vault configuration and effective dates. The frontend bridge and Rust validation must agree.

  • Unit examples: 60 minutes = 60 XP; six 10-minute entries give the same; 70/30 routing yields 42/18; 1 XP splits without creating a second point; 240h unfamiliar completed earns 4,320 bonus.
  • Boundary examples: zero minutes, fractional hours converted once, invalid numbers, 2/10/40-hour rarity thresholds, no estimate, completion above target, partial/reversed milestone, ties in rounding and unknown categories.
  • Integration: one shared award source prevents task/quest duplication; child and parent cannot both pay; retries do not repeat awards; an explicit correction reverses only that award; net bonus never exceeds the committed budget.
  • Historical settings: routing changes and renames preserve prior allocations; archive keeps history; new policy does not reinterpret Legacy; export/import round-trips policy and boundary balances.
  • Filesystem safety: temporary demo-vault copy, byte-identical unrelated notes, conflict recovery, existing-destination refusal, settings failures retaining drafts. Never use personal notes or calendars.
  • E2E: add a category, route it, create a day, record time, accept a milestone, restart and verify totals; archive category; disable/re-enable; switch vaults; inspect settings/error states on the built-in display.
  • Implementation delivery gates: focused tests, npm run check:fast, necessary integration checks, and scheduled full E2E/visual verification. None of these app-implementation gates is claimed by this documentation artifact.
