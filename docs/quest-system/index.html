Epizen / Quest guide

How XP and
quests work

Earn XP for daily activities. Earn a bigger reward when you finish a goal.

Proposed rules, updated 9 November 2026. These changes are not in the app yet.

XP means experience points. They help you see what you have worked on and celebrate what you finish. You choose a task category or quest size, and Epizen works out the points.

01 / Daily activities

Small activities can earn a useful reward

Ten minutes of meditation earns 60 XP. Watching a two-hour film also earns 60 XP. A short practice can be hard to start, so it gets a bigger reward for its first few minutes.

Each category comes with one of these four rules already selected. You can use it straight away or change it in settings.

TypeHow it paysExample
Focus1 XP per minute60 minutes of work = 60 XP
Practice6 XP/min for the first 10 minutes each day, then 1 XP/min10 minutes of meditation = 60 XP
Training1.5 XP/min for the first 30 minutes each day, then 1 XP/min30 minutes of sport = 45 XP
Leisure0.5 XP per minuteA two-hour film = 60 XP

The daily boost is shared

Two five-minute meditations earn 60 XP together. One ten-minute meditation earns the same 60 XP. Another ten minutes that day earns 10 more.

Adding a second meditation category does not restart the boost. All categories that use Practice share it. Training works the same way.

These amounts are starting suggestions. They reflect what we want to encourage. They do not mean that one activity is worth more as a part of your life.

Show the maths and counting rules

For each activity type, add up its minutes on that day's note. The formula is XP = rate × minutes + boost × min(minutes / boost length, 1). Here, min means use the smaller number.

TypeRateBoost / length
Focus10 / none
Practice150 XP / 10 minutes
Training115 XP / 30 minutes
Leisure0.50 / none

For a new session, calculate the day's total again and award only the increase. Practice and Training together add at most 65 boost XP per day. Keep exact amounts behind the scenes: two one-minute Leisure sessions earn 1 XP altogether. Show whole XP to the user. Never add up already-rounded session labels to calculate progress.

Use the date of the daily note. Count overlapping time once. Planning a session earns nothing. If you complete a saved task with a suggested duration, confirm or change that duration before it becomes logged time.

Count entries in a stable order, then divide each reward between its chosen life areas. Keep the exact amounts and round each displayed total to the nearest whole point, with .5 rounded up. Calculate the character total and levels from the exact amounts, not from rounded labels. Editing an old entry recalculates its reward; it does not pay twice. The rounding examples below explain why displayed shares may differ slightly from the displayed total.

02 / Categories

Where your XP goes

A category says what you did, such as Sport or Kaizen. A life area says what it helped, such as Health/Fitness or Personal Growth.

An hour with your dog earns 30 XP: 15 in Fun/Recreation and 15 in Social. You can change the reward rule separately from this 50/50 split.

Kaizen means working on something you want to improve. Business covers work and money. Sport goes toward health. Meditation goes to Spiritual by default, which can mean reflection or finding meaning.

Use the choices that fit you. Reading a novel for fun could use Leisure. Studying a film could use Focus and send XP to Personal Growth. Pick the usual purpose of the category once, rather than choosing a reward for every task.

If you have not chosen a life area yet, the XP appears under Unassigned. Splitting 60 XP between two areas still gives you 60 XP in total.

What you see when adding a task

A first meditation shows: 10 min · about 60 XP · Spiritual. After today's boost is used, another ten minutes shows about 10 XP. The estimate updates when you log the time. You never need to enter a multiplier.

03 / Quests

Finishing a quest should feel worth it

A quest is a goal with a clear finish, such as publishing a website or completing a course project. Its reward should be large enough to make that finish matter.

Choose a size below to see the reward, the usual amount of work, and examples. You can hover, tap, or use the Tab key.

Quest sizeUsual work / suggested hoursReward for finishing
CommonUnder 2 hours / 1 hour100 XP
Rare2 to under 10 hours / 5 hours500 XP
Epic10 to under 40 hours / 20 hours2,000 XP
Legendary40+ hours / 60 hours6,000 XP

To create a quest, write what counts as finished, choose its category or life area, and pick a size. Epizen fills in the reward. It starts with Rare and shows the suggested five hours, so you can change it if needed.

Why the rewards are bigger now

The earlier draft gave a 20-hour Epic only 300 XP. This version gives it 2,000 XP. If those 20 hours use Focus, you also earn 1,200 XP while working. Finish the quest and you have earned 3,200 XP altogether.

The finish now gives more XP than the hours alone. That is deliberate: quests are the big achievements in this system. The reward is a design choice we can adjust after using it, not a proven ideal amount.

If you know how many hours it will take

You can enter your own estimate. The finishing reward is 100 XP per planned hour, rounded to a whole point. A 12-hour quest offers 1,200 XP. A 60-hour quest offers 6,000.

If you leave the estimate empty, Epizen uses the suggested hours for that size. A two-hour Rare and a nine-hour Rare both offer 500 XP until you enter a more precise estimate. This keeps quick setup simple.

With your own estimate, the size label follows the hours: Rare starts at 2, Epic at 10, and Legendary at 40. Changing the label alone does not increase the reward. Clear the estimate to use the suggested amount again.

Weekly and monthly tell you when you want to finish. They do not add XP. A hard monthly quest may be Legendary, but a small monthly goal can still be Common.

Choose a finish you can recognize

“Work on my website” is too vague. “Publish my website and test the contact form” has a clear finish. “Submit an application” is something you can complete; “get hired” also depends on someone else.

For a weekly meditation quest, “finish five ten-minute sessions” is clear. Entering 50 minutes as the planned effort gives an 83 XP finishing reward, on top of the daily XP.

Split big quests into steps

A step is a part you can finish and check. An Epic worth 2,000 XP could have four equal steps, each worth 500 XP. If one step takes more work, give it a larger share when you create the quest.

The steps share the quest's reward. Finishing all four pays 2,000 XP in total. There is no second 2,000 XP payment at the end.

Set the reward before starting. Finishing faster keeps the promised reward. Taking longer earns more daily XP but does not raise the finishing reward. Honest estimates matter more with these larger rewards.

Pausing or giving up keeps the XP you already earned. Reopening a finished quest does not pay again. If you checked something by mistake, undoing it removes that mistaken reward. Splitting an existing quest divides its reward between the new parts.

How partial quest rewards work

Multiply the whole reward by the share finished and round to a whole XP. Pay only the increase since the last update. Half of a 2,000 XP quest is 1,000 XP; three quarters is 1,500. Moving from half to three quarters therefore pays 500 XP.

For a quest with no steps, pay when it is finished. For equal steps, divide finished steps by total steps, stopping at 100%. For unequal steps, their shares must add up to 100%. Time logged does not mark a step finished by itself.

At 100 XP per planned hour, a Focus quest completed on estimate earns about 62.5% of its total XP from finishing and 37.5% from time. Other activity types will have different shares. The app cannot check whether two separately created goals describe the same work, so avoid creating extra quests just to collect another reward.

05 / Year-long goals

A year-long project can have several big rewards

Suppose you plan to spend 240 hours writing a book. The quest offers 24,000 XP for finishing. You could divide it into four steps: outline, first draft, edited draft, and finished book.

If the steps have equal shares, each pays 6,000 XP. The hours may differ, so choose unequal shares if that better matches the work.

What you earnXP
240 logged hours using Focus14,400
Four finished steps, 6,000 each24,000
Total38,400

The whole project includes those four rewards. It does not pay another 24,000 XP afterward. You can call a year-long project “Mythic” if you like; the name adds no extra points.

Only count time spent doing the work. A goal that spans a year but needs five hours is still a five-hour quest. For a project with several activity types, add the daily XP you actually earned and then the quest reward.

How levels work

Keep the app's current levels. Level 2 starts at 1,000 XP, level 3 at 3,000, level 5 at 10,000, and level 10 at 45,000. The book example would take a new character to level 9. Level 9 starts at 36,000 XP.

Level formula

Level L starts at 500 × L × (L − 1) XP. Each new level takes longer than the last. Show progress on the next quest step too, so there is something closer to finish.

06 / Settings

Ready to use, easy to change

A fresh setup comes with the categories, reward rules and XP splits in this guide already selected. Meditation uses Practice. Sport uses Training. Dog uses Leisure and sends half its XP to Fun/Recreation and half to Social. The quest rewards start at 100, 500, 2,000 and 6,000 XP.

Daily notes and Quests are enabled in the proposed new setup, so these defaults work straight away. People can turn them off. Existing vaults keep their saved choices; an app update must not replace them with the new defaults.

Daily notes → Categories

The starter categories are available for new tasks. Adding an empty task row for every category is optional. You can add categories, change their names, reorder them, or hide ones you do not use.

When you remove a category you have used, it moves to an archive. It disappears from new task choices, but its notes and earned XP stay. Old reports still show it. Restoring it brings back the same category. A never-used category can be deleted completely.

Changing a name keeps the category's history. If its note tag changes, the app remembers the old tag too. Creating a new category with a similar name must not take over an archived category's records.

Plugins → Quests → Configure

Each starter category already has a reward type and XP split. Show a short example beside it, such as “10 minutes of meditation = 60 XP.” People can change either setting.

A custom category starts with Focus. The user can choose where its XP goes while adding it, or do that later. Until then, its points stay in Unassigned. No points disappear because a choice is missing.

You can also rename or add life areas. A split can be 33% to one area, 35% to another, and 32% to a third. These add up to 100%. If the total is wrong, show the amount still needed and keep the draft until it is fixed. Allow up to two decimal places in percentages, such as 33.33%.

Removing a used life area archives it and keeps its past XP. Before saving, move any future category shares that point to it to another area or to Unassigned. Quests already started keep their original shares, even if one of those areas is later archived.

Changes affect future rewards

Suppose you earned 1,000 XP in Kaizen, all in Personal Growth. You then change its split to 70% Personal Growth and 30% Career/Work. The old 1,000 XP stays in Personal Growth. After the change starts, a new 100 XP reward gives 70 to Personal Growth and 30 to Career/Work.

Show when a change will start. Tomorrow is the default for daily reward rules and splits, so today's partly used boost stays predictable. Keep older rules so past records still use the right settings. Renaming or hiding a category can happen immediately because it does not change its rewards.

Quests keep the reward and split they had when they started. Correcting a mistaken time entry or completion can still correct its XP. A settings change alone must never take earned XP away.

Save related changes together. If saving fails, keep what the user typed and let them retry. Each vault has its own settings. Resetting to defaults shows a preview and changes future rewards only; installing an update never silently resets anything.

Turning Quests off hides its views while keeping notes, settings and earned XP. Turning it back on shows the existing records without paying them again. Quests needs Daily notes, called Periodic notes in settings, enabled.

Show whole XP

People see 33 XP instead of 33.3, and 34 XP instead of 33.6. An exact half rounds up, so 33.5 becomes 34. This applies to task rewards, life areas, quest progress and totals.

Keep the exact numbers behind the scenes. Add those numbers first, then round the result for display. For example, ten rewards of 0.4 XP add up to 4 XP, even though each one alone would display as 0 XP. The fractions are not lost.

Share of 101 XPExact amountShown in the app
33%33.33 XP33 XP
35%35.35 XP35 XP
32%32.32 XP32 XP
Total101 XP101 XP

The three displayed areas above add up to 100, while the total shows 101. That is normal rounding. Keep the accurate total and offer a short explanation: “Each number is rounded. Small differences are normal.” Do not change someone's share to make the rounded labels match.

The same rule applies to days, weeks and months: calculate from the exact records, then round. Sorting, filtering, restarting the app or changing the display must not change earned XP. Levels use the exact total too, so a rounded display cannot unlock a level early. Near a boundary, the level explanation can say that the next level needs a little more XP.

How to keep settings safe as the app grows

Give each category and life area a permanent ID separate from its name. Keep archived IDs, old tags, dated reward rules and dated splits. Each reward must be traceable to the rule and split that produced it. Keep this information when exporting or importing settings, so another installation can show the same history.

Use exact decimal or fraction calculations for stored XP. Store percentages as hundredths of a percent and require their sum to be exactly 10,000. Do not rely on repeated floating-point rounding. Reverse the original exact amount when correcting an award.

Use one calculation for every screen. Saving settings and their history must succeed together, and a retry must not create a second change. An older app that cannot understand a newer rules version should keep the data and explain the problem rather than replacing it with defaults.

Historical task XP can be rebuilt from notes and the rules that applied on their dates. Preserve quest rules from when the quest started. None of this needs a database or hidden files beside notes. Use the existing settings storage and readable Markdown export. Category removal must not delete or rewrite the source notes.

07 / Ideas from games

Where these ideas came from

Dungeons & Dragons

The 2014 rules explain what Easy, Medium, Hard and Deadly encounters mean. They also give XP tables. The useful idea for Epizen is to explain each label with examples, so “Legendary” tells you what kind of work to expect. D&D encounter rules.

Habitica

Habitica has four task difficulty choices: Trivial, Easy, Medium and Hard. A short list makes choosing easier. In Epizen, the category makes most of that choice for you. Habitica documentation.

Guild Wars 2

The Wizard's Vault has daily, weekly and special goals. Its designers also explain how keeping cosmetic rewards available later reduces pressure to play on a deadline. Epizen borrows the idea of daily activities alongside bigger goals, with no lost XP for being late. ArenaNet's explanation.

The XP numbers in this guide are our own choices. These games do not prove that meditation should earn 60 XP or that a Legendary quest should earn 6,000.

Try it for a month

Notice whether the points help you start useful activities and finish goals. If you find yourself adding tasks just for points, change future defaults. Keep points already earned.

Look at time spent, goals finished and how you feel about each life area. A low XP score can mean you did not log much. It does not prove you are bad at that part of life. You do not need equal scores everywhere.

There are no late penalties, expiring XP or extra rewards for an unbroken streak. Missing a day should make it easy to return the next day.

08 / Existing notes

Keep the XP you already earned

This is a proposed update. The app still uses its existing rules. The new Dog split and larger quest rewards have only been changed in this guide.

When this is added to the app, existing vaults should keep their old rules until you choose to switch. The app should show a start date and keep the old XP as an opening balance. Old deadlines must not keep subtracting from that balance.

Keep old quests under their original rules unless you choose to convert them. Count rewards already paid during that conversion, so nothing pays twice. If old character and life-area totals differ, show the difference as an old-balance adjustment rather than guessing where it came from.

Keep notes as ordinary Markdown files. Store rules and their start dates in vault settings, with an option to export and import them as readable Markdown. Opening a note must not change it. If a note changes while an edit is being saved, keep the unsaved text and offer recovery.

Developer notes and checks

Use one scoring calculation for Home, daily notes and weekly/monthly totals. Keep quest rewards and completed steps in the quest module, and saved rules in settings.

  • Check 10 minutes of meditation = 60 XP, two five-minute sessions = 60, and 20 minutes = 70. Two categories sharing Practice must share its boost.
  • Check a two-hour film = 60 XP, an hour of Training = 75, and two one-minute Leisure sessions = 1. An hour of Dog must split 30 XP into 15 and 15.
  • Check all four quest sizes, custom hours, 240 hours = 24,000 reward XP, partial steps and undo. Parent quests and their steps must not pay twice.
  • Check new setup defaults, existing settings on upgrade, archive/restore, old tags, dated splits, 33/35/32 and decimal percentages, normal rounding, tiny repeated rewards, unchanged past XP, export/import, failed saves, vault switching and turning Quests off and on.
  • Use temporary copies of demo-vault. Prove that unrelated files stay byte-for-byte unchanged and that failed saves keep the user's text.
  • Test the full app journey: add a category, log time, read quest help with the keyboard, finish a step, restart and check the totals. Use the built-in display.
  • Before shipping app changes, run the relevant tests and npm run check:fast, plus required app and visual checks. The guide's calculator tests do not replace these.
